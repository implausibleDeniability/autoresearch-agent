Despite physicians consistently reporting that all LASEOs are the same, differences in perceptions emerged
Fentanyl has a distinct brand image
Fentanyl's unique delivery system allows physicians to clearly articulate benefits and define appropriate patients (e.g. cancer patients)
Barriers to use are largely functional focused around the transdermal application and 3-day dosing
Oral LA SEOs differ in negative perceptions
OxyContin and morphine loyalists both view their drug of choice as powerful and effective
Morphine loyalists avoid OxyContin, perceiving a higher risk of diversion that does not compensate for additional benefits
OxyContin loyalists view morphine as a "dirtier" drug with more side effects and inconsistency between generic formulations
Positive images of Opana ER, Avinza, Ka-dian counterbalanced by barriers to Rx
A majority of physicians do not use these products due to a combination of low awareness, poor formulary coverage, sparse detailing, and a lack of experience
Experts appreciate the pharmacokinetic profile and perceive lack of euphoria of Opana ER
Non-experts value these LA SEOs for their low level of awareness among general public
Economics can trump brand preference
Perception of increased managed care control in LA SEO class
More restrictive tiering and prior-authorization qualifications, along with quantity restrictions per Rx for OxyContin
Morphine most frequently chosen as an affordable, effective alternative
Although cheaper, many physicians are not comfortable using methadone

Summary of sensitivity to detailing
Increased detailing to PCPs and top specialties could yield additional ~$106M in Gross Sales and ~$48M in contribution, requiring a further ~120 reps vs. current salesforce (before planned increase of 50 reps)

We have used sensitivities to detailing from dynamic analysis shared at last meeting to assess potential ROI upon an increase in PCP coverage and frequency. This uncovered a potential additional opportunity of ~$86 M gross sales and ~$38 M contribution
Extending similar analysis to specialists only uncovered a potential additional opportunity of ~$21 M additional gross sales and ~$10 M additional contribution, suggesting salesforce is already covering most of the high potential specialists
Overall additional contribution opportunity can be captured in three increments:
 ~60% of additional contribution (~$28M) by targeting next wave of 50 reps to increasing coverage and frequency to specialists and high LA SEO PCPs with > 20% OER share
A further ~25% of the opportunity (~$12M) requiring ~40 reps consists of increasing frequency in remaining PCPs already being detailed, as well as extending coverage in other PCPs segments with either OER share >50% or high LA SEO Rx
Remainder ~15% of the opportunity (~$8M) could be captured using ~30 reps to  increase coverage of low LA SEO physicians with <50% OER share
Next steps for implementation would involve reviewing balance of additional calls across territories and producing target lists excluding no-detail physicians

OER TRx growth in 2008 partly driven by LTC, due to:
An increase in LA SEO TRx in LTC of 23%
OER share of LA SEO increase within LTC of 1.5p.p.
OER only major molecule with discontinuation as % of TRx increase since 05/08, reaching similar level as competitors; even more so for generic with increase from ~15% to 25-30%
Increase in OER discontinuation coincided with increase in US unemployment rate
Physician qualitative research points to patients finding ways to deal with economic crisis (e.g., ‘stretching' Rxs)
At the same time, OxyContin news volume remained stable
Decline of non-OER LA SEOs in Humana of ~6% in line with opioids overall, suggesting potential decline in lives covered
However, OER suffered a disproportionate decline of 24%, likely as a consequence of removal from Part D formulary;
Similar trend in most other payers with LA SEOs decline
Physician qualitative research confirms trend of increasing MC control over LA SEOs (e.g., formulary, QLs)
