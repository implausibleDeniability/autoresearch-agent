18

Main adjustments need to account for consumer overstatement, awareness and distribution level
Detail in following pages
Source: McKinsey Consumer and Shopper Insights (CSI) Practice
Type of adjustment
Description
Consumer overstatement
Respondents typically overstate their intention to buy a product
There are no real life budget constraints when taking a survey
Interviewer biases may be present (in interviewer administered surveys)
Respondents use scales differently vs. their actual intentions
Overstatement varies by
Category, e.g., bigger ticket items have higher overstatement since real decision is more complex
Regions, countries and demographic groups
Data collection method, e.g., online vs phone surveys
These responses (incl. those saying ‘definitely will buy') must be discounted
Awareness
Since we show the concept to everyone in the survey, we assume the product will have 100% awareness
Ideally, adjust assumptions based on intended marketing investment
Distribution level
Concept testing assumes 100% availability to the product, which may be unrealistic if:
Launch will only take place in certain geographies
Product will not be available in all relevant channels
Ideally, adjust assumptions based on intended distribution plan and information on the category
Other
Other potential adjustments may include inertia (such as in several categories where a purchase is not necessarily warranted) & execution assumptions (e.g., promotional introductory plans, advertising execution/appeal)
