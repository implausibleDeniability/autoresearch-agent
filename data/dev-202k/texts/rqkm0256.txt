Potential impact of ADF MS Contin on generics
Discussion document
Document type | Date
PROPRIETARY AND CONFIDENTIAL

Executive summary
We considered the potential impact of ADF MS Contin on generics in two scenarios:
Non-ADF generic morphine sulfate will be considered as AB-rated to new ADF version of MS Contin
Non-ADF generic morphine sulfate will no longer be AB-rated to new ADF version of MS Contin
If generic morphine sulfate is still considered as AB-rated to ADF version of MS Contin, Rhodes's generic morphine sulfate volume is likely to maintain but with <1% volume shift to the new ADF MS Contin
The volume shift to the new ADF MS Contin is most likely coming from the new patients (<10% total patients), but majority of the HCPs still have the preference of prescribing non-ADF version in generic name (>90%), which potentially leads to <1% volume shift to new ADF MS Contin
In the scenario of not being AB-rated, apart from the volume shift to the new ADF version, generic morphine sulfate may lose a small amount of volume (<1% total morphine market) in scripts prescribed with MS Contin brand name since generics are not substitutable without AB-rating
Rhodes will be able to maintain the most of its generic volume if it is AB-rated to new ADF version of MS Contin
Even if current non-ADF generics will no longer be considered as AB-rated to the new ADF version of MS Contin, which is a more likely scenario, it will only impact <1% of total morphine market, because majority of physicians are used to prescribe in molecule name of morphine sulfate (accounts for ~99% of scripts) instead of in brand name

Other assumptions and findings highlights
MS Contin can be replaced with ADF without changing the brand name, but the manufacturer has to change the package and inform the HCPs
If MS Contin is replaced with ADF formulation, generics manufacture will have to apply for AB-rating to the ADF version
Non-ADF version is unlikely to be considered as AB-rated to ADF due to:
Difference in activity
May steer patients away from ADF version
If non-ADF generics are not AB-rated to ADF MS Contin, pharmacists are not allowed to switch the script to generics
Pharmacist will have to clarify with HCP on ADF, or patient will have to go back to HCP to change the script
The potential drivers that may shift volume from generics to new ADF version of MS Contin include:
Improved formulary coverage
Growth in MS Contin written prescription due to increased awareness among HCPs
The formulary access of new ADF version of MS Contin may slightly be better than original MS Contin given ADF label and ADF coverage mandate in 5 states

Legislation mandating abuse-deterrent formulations of opioids have been introduced in nearly half of US states, though only 5 states have passed legislation into law
Most of the unpassed bills have stalled in state legislature committees
Legislation mandating ADF coverage also generally forbids step-through non-ADF restrictions
Legislation generally includes language that permits the use of prior authorizations for ADFs as long as they are the same or less restrictive as the non-ADF versions
Legislation generally includes language stating payors cannot achieve compliance by increasing patient cost sharing or by issuing disincentives for prescribers.
