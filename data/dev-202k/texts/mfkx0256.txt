BuTrans Label - Warning and Precautions
5.6 Congenital or Acquired QT Prolongation
"A positive controlled study of effects of BuTrans on the QT interval in normal subjects, demonstrated the therapeutic dose of BuTrans™ 10 mcg/h had no effect on QTci. However, the supratherapeutic dose of BuTrans 40 mcg/h (given as two Bu Trans 20 mcg/h patches) was observed to prolong the QTci interval to an extent comparable to the prolongation seen with 400 mg of moxifloxacin [see CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY (12.2)]. These observations should be considered in clinical decisions when prescribing BuTrans to patients with hypokalemia or clinically unstable cardiac disease, including: unstable atrial fibrillation, symptomatic bradycardia, unstable congestive heart failure, or active myocardial ischemia. Patients with a history of Long QT Syndrome or an immediate family member with this condition, or those taking Class IA antiarrhythmic medications (eg, quinidine, procainamide, disopyramide) or Class III antiarrhythmic medications (eg, sotalol, amiodarone, dofetilide) should consider the risk of adding BuTrans treatment."

Conclusions
Low hERG inhibition at therapeutic dose
Positive TQT study (BUP1011)
ECG data present some signals of QT prolongation potential
Clinical studies did not identify a profile that signals proarrhythmic potential
Postmarketing data reveal a benign cardiac safety profile
Drug interaction with 3A4 and 2D6 inhibitors is unlikely a serious concern as the therapeutic doses  are low
