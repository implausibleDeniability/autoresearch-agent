What will reps see on the prescriber selection screen to help them with prescriber selection (e.g.   what fields)?  The prescriber selection screen will show   * Prescriber information, including name, IMS ID and address information  * Brand target- which brand to deliver as a primary call  * Value Decile for each product- Similar to SEO or extended release opioid decile, though the   Value Decile includes information on the Impact Targeting factors (e.g., managed care   access, NBRx writing)   * TRx for each product   * OxyContin and Butrans potential- indicates overall potential script volume for each   prescriber, based on impact targeting factors  * OxyContin NBRx % potential- the estimated % of future prescribing potential that comes   from new to brand scripts. This is an important Impact Factor because NBRx indicate new   business   * Tramadol/Nucynta TRx- The scripts of Tramadol and Nucynta.  Important targeting factor for   Butrans  * Calls in the last 12 months       2. How is customer value determined?  For each product, prescribers are valued by taking into account historical prescription volume and   then adjusting for additional factors.     For OxyContin, these additional factors are overall opioid market volume, managed care access,   generic preference, and new-to-OxyContin brand prescribing volume.     For Butrans, these additional factors are opioids for moderate pain, managed care access, generic   preference, and new-to-brand prescribing volume for ERO overall.     These factors allow us to estimate the prescribing potential for each prescriber.     3. What 'trumps' what in factoring?  For OxyContin, the first factor applied is OxyContin TRx volume for that prescriber.  Then, potential   is layered in based on IR + ER TRx volume.  Then this value is adjusted based on managed care access   and generic penetration.  Finally, if historic NBRx writing for OxyContin is higher than these factors,   the NBRx "wins".  In most cases, the strongest influence in determining value is the historical   OxyContin TRx volume.    For Butrans, the factoring approach is similar to that for OxyContin. The first factor applied is   Butrans historical script volume. Then, future prescribing potential is layered in based on IR + ER   products, with more weight given to moderate pain treatments such as Tramadol and Nucynta. This   value is adjusted based on managed care access and generic penetration. Lastly, historic new to   brand scripts written for all ERO products (excluding OxyContin) is taken into account. If NBRx   writing is higher than the additional script potential of all the other factors, the NBRx factor 'trumps'   the other factors.     4. Are deciles national or regional?  Deciles are national. The prescribing potential of each prescriber is estimated using the impact   targeting factors. The "value deciles" are created using the prescribing potential of all prescribers   (e.g., nationally). Hence, there will be some territories with more high "Value decile" prescribers   than others, since value deciles are created nationally, not by region or territory.  However, despite   the national deciling, the prescribers in the suggested list include the highest value prescribers for   each territory.     5. Are prior auths/step edits factored into the managed care factor?  Yes - within the managed care factor, we consider preferred branded tier without restrictions (e.g.,   no prior authorizations or step edits), preferred branded tier with restrictions (e.g., with prior   authorization and/or step edits), tier 3, and tier 4 or not covered.     6. Are there cases where reps should go see HCPs >24 times? Why is 24 the max number of calls?  If reps are able to make the recommended number of calls to all other prescribers on their list, then   reps can make more than 24 calls on the HCPs they choose. While there may be benefits of calling   more than 24 times on certain high-value prescribers, on average reps will see diminishing returns   past 24 calls and their call effort will yield greater benefit on other prescribers.    7. On April 1, 2014,   the Q1 target list will go away, and the Q2 target list is the only list available.   8. Why does the call list need to be locked? Why can't prescribers be added mid-quarter?  Repeated calls over an extended period of time are needed to produce TRx increase in prescribers.   Hence, committing to a call list for a quarter allows reps to concentrate their calls on the high value   customers over the course of three months rather than switching customers too often. Moreover,   as incentive compensation for OxyContin is now focused on TRx generated by prescribers on the   final selected list, it is essential to keep a locked list.     9. How was the 1,536 calls arrived at on the target selection screen and is the annual objective for   calls?   The 1,536 calls is for prescriber selection only - each rep must choose enough prescribers such that   the annual calls annually sums to 1,536 (e.g. a T-24 counts for 24 calls, a T-12 counts for 12, etc...). It   does not represent an annual call target. Reps and managers should focus on the average calls per   day goal of 7.5 days.      The 1,536 annual call number was derived based on 7.5 calls/day with a national average of 205   days on territory (excluding long-term absences).  The importance of the annual 1,536 calls is to   ensure that each and every rep has a prescriber selection that gives them sufficient optimized   prescriber selections for a quarter, even if they spent minimal time off territory.      We realize that reps have individualized days on territory which vary according to vacation   entitlement, training plans and other factors that cause a rep to be off territory. Thus, reps will not   be held to a total annual calls target, only to a calls/day target.    2. Will no-sees be on the Q2 list?  Yes. Reps will find prescribers they marked as "no see" at the bottom of the list. It is important for   reps to keep track of the high value "no see" prescribers, in case office access may change.     3. These calls will count   toward the FCR metric for total calls/day, but not on total calls/day on final selected prescribers.      4. Can reps increase frequencies?  Reps can increase frequencies up to the "optimal frequency" level indicated on the suggested list,   only in cases of limited sees. Prescribers marked as "limited see" will be pre-populated with a lower   than optimal frequency to reflect the "limited see" status. In these cases, reps will be able to   increase this frequency back to optimal. For example, if optimal frequency for a prescriber is T24,   and reps marked the prescriber as limited see 6, then the pre-populated frequency in the "selected   frequency" column is T6; Frequency can be increased back to a T12 or T24.  Otherwise, reps cannot   increase frequencies above the optimal level.     5. Will a prescriber 'disappear' if not selected? What if the rep needs to go back?
